# M3 — grammar and punctuation

- Source: https://m3.material.io/foundations/content-design/style-guide/grammar-and-punctuation
- Captured: 2026-08-13
- Scope: English UX text. Google follows AP style. Condensed in our own words, not a copy of the page.

## Periods
Leave the period off a single sentence — labels, tooltips, bulleted lists, dialog body, linked text.
Use one for two or more sentences, or for a long sentence where it helps.

## Contractions
Use them; they scan faster. Keep "do not" when the sentence is a warning and needs weight.

## Commas
Serial (Oxford) comma in lists of three or more, but not before "&".
Commas go inside the quotation marks.
Use a comma in numbers from 1,000 to 1 million. No comma in an address, a radio frequency or a year.
Above a million, round and abbreviate — "3M" — when the point is the scale, not the exact figure.

## Colons
No colon in a heading that introduces a list. Use one for a list inside body text.

## Exclamation points
Rarely — they read as shouting. Only for a greeting or a real celebration, never for an empty state
or an everyday task.

## Ellipses
Only for an action in progress, or text cut short. No space before the character.
Never on a menu item or a button that opens a dialog or starts a process.

## Parentheses
For defining an acronym or a technical term, or citing a source.
Not for side notes — if the information matters, put it in the sentence.

## Ampersands
"&" is for headlines, column and table headers, navigation labels and buttons, where space is tight.
Spell out "and" in sentences, before the last item of a 3+ list, and in email subjects.

## Dashes
Three kinds: em dash "—", en dash "–", hyphen "-".
Avoid the em dash in UI text; a comma, a period or a second sentence reads better.
En dash for a range, no spaces around it: "9 AM–Noon".

## Hyphens
No spaces around a hyphen. Also used for negative numbers, "-100". Check AP style when unsure.
- Hyphenate an adjective phrase: "case-by-case basis", "best-in-class performance".
  Not needed for proper nouns or familiar phrases: "cell phone number", "chocolate chip cookie".
- Hyphenate a noun phrase built from a verb plus an adverb: "sign-off", "drive-through", "go-ahead".
  Also an adjective phrase acting as a noun: "higher-up", "most-read". Long ones are always
  hyphenated: "jack-of-all-trades".
- Do not hyphenate a verb phrase: "check in to the room", but "the next check-in".

## Italics
Hard to read, so use bold for emphasis. Italics only for a single word or a title, never a whole
sentence.

## Caps
No caps blocks — they are not accessible. Sentence case for all product text.
