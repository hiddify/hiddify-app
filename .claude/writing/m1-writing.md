# M1 — writing style

- Source: https://m1.material.io/style/writing.html
- Captured: 2026-08-13
- Older Material site. Condensed in our own words, not a copy of the page.

## Voice and tone
- **Friendly.** Casual and plain: "MyApp isn't responding. Do you want to close it?"
- **Humble.** Show the feature, don't oversell it: "All your savings in one place", not "Great deals
  at places you'll love."
- **Inviting.** Lead with the benefit, leave out the mechanics and the caveats.
- **Positive.** "Try again" beats "The action failed."
- **Essential.** Only what the user needs. Sometimes the best string is no string.

## Addressing the user
- **"you" / "your"** for almost everything: "Quickly open the camera without unlocking your screen."
- **"I" / "my"** to mark ownership of a decision: "I agree to follow the Google Terms of Service."
- **Avoid "we".** Say what the user can do. Exception: a real person is doing the work —
  "We'll review your appeal and respond within a few days."

## Word choice
- **Concise.** Short sentences, scannable pieces.
- **Present tense.** Simple verb forms when you must reach into past or future.
- **Simple.** Cut the polite run-up: "Save changes?", not "Would you like to save your changes?"
- **No jargon.** "Turn on Location History", not "Enable Location History."
- **Numerals.** "You have 3 messages."
- **Consistent verbs.** Pick "Remove photo" and use it everywhere.
- **Objective first.** "To remove a photo from this album, drag it to the trash."
- **No absolutes.** "Your circle names aren't shared", not "We'll never share your circle names."

## Capitalization
Sentence case: "Search settings", not "Search Settings". First word and proper nouns only.

## Punctuation
- No period after a single sentence — labels, hover text, bulleted lists, a one-sentence dialog.
  Periods for two or more sentences.
- No colon after a label: "Share with", not "Share with:".
- Contractions read better: "it's", "can't", "wouldn't".
- Avoid exclamation points; they shout. Greetings and congratulations excepted.
- En dash for ranges: "3–5 kg". Ellipsis for an action under way: "Downloading…" — but not on a
  menu item or button that opens a dialog.
- Curly quotation marks and apostrophes, not straight ones.

## Writing for a global audience
- Plain English that survives translation, whatever the reader's schooling or background.
- Careful with "left" and "right": the layout mirrors in right-to-left languages.
- Gender-neutral "they" when gender is unknown. Never "his/her". When a translation genuinely needs
  gender, give the translator instructions and use the ICU APIs.
